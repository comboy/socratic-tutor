# Prepared visuals

Drawings and animations made ahead of the lesson, so that when the moment comes the tutor drops
one in with a line. For tutors (finding and using one), for whoever prepares them (the `visuals`
background job, a subagent, a tutor between replies), and for developers.

## Why

A drawing written inside a reply is composed in seconds while the learner waits, and nobody sees
it before they do; one evening four went out wrong and were corrected after posting. A drawing
made with no clock running can be rendered, looked at step by step, checked against the science
by a second pair of eyes, and animated where a process is the point. A learner who learns from
pictures first gets a better picture, and the tutor's reply gets faster, not slower.

## Where things live

Per topic, in the learner's data (never in the code repo: it is topic content):

    data/topics/<slug>/visuals/<name>.js    one visual (format below)
    data/topics/<slug>/visuals/_kit.js      the topic's drawing kit: how recurring things are drawn
    data/topics/<slug>/visuals/STYLE.md     the same conventions in words, with reasons
    data/topics/<slug>/visuals/requests.md  the queue: what tutors wished they had
    data/topics/<slug>/visuals/index.json   generated by `visuals check`; don't edit

Topic-neutral code is in the app: `web/viz/visual.js` (loads and runs one), `web/viz/scene.js`
(the stage, the steps, the controls), `app/visuals.js` (the commands).

## Using one (tutor)

    ![Watch the green sugar: which side does it end on?](visual:budding-and-fusion)

On its own line, in a message, a term page or a wiki page. The caption is yours: say what to look
at, or ask. Parameters go after `?`, joined by `&`:

- `step=3`: open on the end of step 3 (a still of that moment; the controls still work).
- `upto=3`: the learner can't go past step 3. Show the start and ask what happens next, then post
  the full one. A prediction is worth more than a viewing.
- `text=0`: hide the step captions (when your question is what the caption would say).
- `hl=lumen,cargo`: light up those legend keys in the drawing and your prose.
- `play=1`: start moving at once. The default is a still that waits for the learner, because
  motion beside text pulls the eye off the text.
- anything else is the visual's own (its `params:` line says).

`bin/learn resume` lists the ready visuals for the focus, what comes next and the open reviews,
each with what it shows, when to use it and its steps. `bin/learn visuals find <words>` searches
all of them; `visuals show <name>` prints everything about one. `post` refuses a `visual:` that
doesn't exist and tells you when one is still a draft.

Wished you had one? One line, then carry on teaching:

    bin/learn visuals want "the repressor falling off when lactose binds: they keep thinking it is destroyed" --skill 6.1 --ref <message id>

A good request says what it should show and what the learner got wrong without it.

The learner sees the same controls on every scene: Back, Play/Next (one step, then it stops so the
caption can be read), Play all, a dot per step; ← → and space when it has focus. Under each visual
is its `simplified:` line, tagged "Schematic": a cartoon says that it is one.

## Making one

A visual is a file `visuals/<name>.js` (name: a-z, 0-9, `-`): a header comment, then the body of
`function (el, ctx, d3, opts, kit)`, like a `viz js` block with two more things in scope.

```js
/* visual
title: Budding and fusion: which side is which
skills: 3.4, 2.2
status: draft
shows: one line: what the learner sees happen
use: one line: the moment a tutor should reach for it (which confusion, which question)
params: step=1..5 · upto=N · hl=lumen
simplified: what it leaves out or distorts, in the learner's language (shown under the drawing)
sources: where the numbers and shapes come from
*/
const sc = ctx.scene({ w: 760, h: 360, steps: [
  { id: 'bud',  title: 'A vesicle buds', text: 'markdown caption, **terms** get their hover cards', dur: 2.5, hl: ['cargo'] },
  { id: 'fuse', title: 'It fuses with the cell membrane', text: '…' } ] });
kit.cytosol(sc);
const g = sc.svg.append('g');
// …build every shape once…
sc.draw(f => {
  // …then place everything from f, for ANY moment:
  const b = sc.ease(f.at('bud'));     // 0 before the step, 0→1 while it plays, 1 after
  vesicle.attr('cx', sc.lerp(100, 400, b));
  sc.show(label, f.at('fuse'));       // fade in during the last step
});
el.append(ctx.legend(['membrane', 'outside', 'cytosol', 'cargo']));
```

**One draw function of time, no chained transitions.** That is what lets the learner step back
and replay, the tutor embed a single moment, and the checker render each step. `f` has: `t`
(0…number of steps), `i` and `id` (current step), `p` (progress in it), `at(id)`, `seg(id, a, b)`
(progress within the part a…b of a step, to sequence things inside one), `clock` (seconds of
running time, for jiggle), `still` (true in the headless check).

`sc` also has: `svg` (d3 selection, viewBox `0 0 w h`), `layer()`, `label(g, x, y, text, {anchor,
size, bold, muted, key})` (theme colour, halo; `\n` for lines), `inlabel` (dark text on a filled
shape), `arrow(g, [x1,y1], [x2,y2], {color, bow, dash, key})`, `show(sel, 0…1)`, `lerp`, `ease`,
`clamp`, `mix(colorA, colorB, p)`, `along(path, p)`, `rng(seed)`, `wobble(time, n)` (smooth
seeded jiggle in −1…1), `control(node)` (a slider under the caption: `sc.control(ctx.slider(…))`,
then `sc.redraw()` in its handler), `ambient: true` in the scene options (the clock runs while
the visual is on screen, for motion that is the point, like thermal jiggle; use it sparingly).
A visual without steps (a slider is the point) passes `steps: []` and gets a stage with no
player. `opts` holds the embed's parameters. Everything in `ctx` from `viz js` works
(`ctx.color(key)`, `ctx.key(el, key)`, `ctx.legend([...])`, `ctx.md`, `ctx.slider`).

`kit` is the topic's own: open `_kit.js` and STYLE.md before drawing, and draw recurring things
with it. If the thing you need will recur, add it to the kit, not to your visual.

What makes one good (from the first batch):

- **One idea per visual**, the one a learner gets wrong. Three to six steps. Each caption says
  what just happened and why, in two or three sentences, with the field's real names.
- **Every state must be readable as a still.** Labels sit next to what they name at every step,
  and nothing overlaps at any step, not only the last.
- **Accurate, and honest about the rest.** Real numbers where there are any (sizes, counts,
  rates), with the source in `sources:`. What is left out or exaggerated goes in `simplified:`.
  Random motion is seeded (`sc.rng`, `sc.wobble`), so a checked frame is the frame the learner sees.
- **The legend's colours, the kit's shapes.** A new recurring thing gets a legend colour first
  (`bin/learn legend set`), an unused one.
- **Width.** The message column is about 780 px; a 760-wide viewBox is drawn 1:1. Text 12 px or
  more.

## Checking and marking ready

    bin/learn visuals [t] check <name> [--mid] [--with "upto=2"]    # ~10 s
    bin/learn visuals [t] ready <name> --reviewed "who looked at what"

`check` draws the visual headless as the app does, in both themes: as embedded with no
parameters, then at the end of every step (`--mid`: half-way through each too), measures what
`viz-check` measures (hidden, overlapping, cut-off, unreadable or tiny text, errors), saves a PNG
per frame and theme, and records the result in `index.json` for the file as it is now. Then
**look at the frames** (Read shows PNGs): the machine can't tell whether step 3 shows what its
caption says.

A visual is `draft` until `ready` is run, and `ready` refuses unless the last check is of the
current file (and kit) and clean (`--despite "why"` for a false alarm) and the header has
`shows`, `use` and `simplified`. `--reviewed` is one line on who checked what; the useful
second look is a fresh agent given only the frames, the captions and the question "what would a
learner wrongly conclude from this?", plus a check of the facts and numbers against a source.
Tutors see only ready visuals in `resume`. Changing the kit makes every check stale on purpose:
`check --all`, look again.

## The queue

`requests.md` in the topic is what tutors asked for (`visuals want`). The `visuals` background
job (`docs/jobs/visuals.md`) works through it and also looks ahead on its own: the path in
"Resume here", the skills after the focus, the reviews that keep failing, misconceptions a
picture would fix. The planner's snapshot shows, per topic, how many are ready, wanted and which
upcoming skills have none.
